# Spoken narration guidance

## Preserve the contract

Keep product facts, control labels, values, and supported claims aligned with the approved story. Maintain one canonical spoken-text field; captions should match that text. Keep provider-specific pronunciation hints or performance markup separate so they cannot silently alter the claim or leak into captions.

## Write one lesson, not an action log

Before writing lines, answer in plain language: what the viewer is trying to decide, what evidence is already visible, what changes, and what the finished state lets them do. Read the lines in sequence without the action list. They should still sound like one explanation with an opening and a payoff.

Cover active interactions close to when they happen. State what is being added, chosen, typed, moved, or saved and why the choice matters or what visible result follows. A short line may cover adjacent actions when each action has its own clear spoken anchor. Do not leave visible clicks, typing, or drag actions silent, but do not turn speech into a duplicate control-label transcript either.

## Make it sound spoken

- Put one useful thought in each breath; split a line if it carries two unrelated explanations.
- Use ordinary words, contractions, and varied sentence lengths where they fit the audience.
- Use first person for a genuine editorial choice or point of view, not every button press.
- Name the visible chart, field, report, value, or saved state. Avoid vague filler such as “now becomes,” “shape the view,” or “operational signal.”
- Read adjacent beats together and remove repeated setup, identical sentence starters, empty praise, and automation stage directions.
- Keep pronunciation notes outside the canonical caption text.

Timing is an editorial question: if a sentence does not fit naturally before or during the action, shorten it or allow more screen time. Do not speed up the voice to satisfy a convenient word-per-minute target.

## Proof checklist

- Every spoken factual claim has current evidence.
- Every active action has one clear spoken anchor.
- The through-line survives when the words are read without the action labels.
- Caption text matches the canonical speech.
- The spoken thought fits the visible state and gives the viewer time to understand it.
