# Brief checklist

Create or update the brief in the run workspace before drafting a storyboard. Keep it short and decision-oriented; do not collect information that will not affect the demo.

## Record

- Viewer and the task or decision they care about.
- Product surface and current starting state, with links or references to evidence.
- One primary outcome and the visible proof that demonstrates it.
- Required feature behavior, including any state-changing action that must be shown.
- Non-goals, intended audience/channel, accessibility needs, privacy boundaries, and hard delivery constraints.
- Assumptions and unresolved facts, each with an owner or a question.

## Ask well

- Ask one question, then wait for the answer before asking another.
- Give two or three practical choices when that makes the decision easier. State one recommendation and why the current evidence supports it.
- Do not make the recommendation sound like an already-approved decision.
- Cache a decision with its question, selected answer, rationale, timestamp, and the evidence or brief version it depends on. Reuse it only while those inputs remain current.
- Skip questions the request or evidence already resolves. Ask for clarification only when the missing answer would materially change the story or the safe scope.

## Product grounding

Use evidence the project actually provides: current source, tests, current docs, or a live app the user has authorized you to inspect. A requested claim without supporting evidence stays unconfirmed. Do not invent metrics, customer outcomes, capabilities, or interface states to make the story more compelling.

Mark evidence as `confirmed`, `inferred`, or `unknown`. For an inference, state the observation it follows from. For an unknown, either remove the claim or request the smallest evidence needed.
